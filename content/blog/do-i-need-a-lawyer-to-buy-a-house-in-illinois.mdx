If you are shopping for a home in the southern Chicago suburbs, you have probably wondered whether you actually need to hire an attorney or whether your real estate agent can handle everything. Illinois law does not require a buyer to retain counsel, but in practice, attorney involvement is the norm in nearly every residential transaction in Cook, Will, and DuPage counties. At [Tommalieh Law](/orland-park/residential-real-estate-lawyer), we represent buyers throughout the process, from the moment an offer is accepted through the day keys change hands, and we regularly see what happens when buyers skip legal review and later wish they had not.

## Illinois Real Estate Contracts and the Attorney Review Period

Illinois is somewhat unusual in how it structures the negotiation of home purchase contracts. Rather than having attorneys draft the contract from scratch, most buyers and sellers sign a standard form contract prepared by their real estate agents, often the Multi-Board Residential Real Estate Contract used throughout the Chicago area. That contract becomes binding once both parties sign, but it includes a built-in attorney review and inspection period, typically five business days, during which either side's lawyer can propose modifications, object to contract terms, or in some cases terminate the agreement outright.

This structure means the initial signed contract is not necessarily the final word. It is a starting point that attorneys can still shape. A buyer's attorney will send a formal review letter within the deadline addressing financing contingencies, the timeline for the home inspection, earnest money handling, personal property that should be included in the sale, and any language that unfairly shifts risk to the buyer. Missing this window can mean losing the ability to renegotiate anything beyond what an inspection contingency allows.

### Why the Timeline Matters

Because attorney review runs on a strict business-day clock, buyers who wait to contact a lawyer until after signing often compress an already tight window. We recommend lining up counsel before you make an offer, or at the very latest, the same day your offer is accepted, so your attorney has the full review period to work with rather than a day or two at the end.

## What a Buyer's Attorney Actually Does For You

A common misconception is that a real estate attorney simply shows up at closing to notarize documents. In reality, most of the value a buyer's lawyer provides happens well before closing day.

### Reviewing and Negotiating the Purchase Contract

Your attorney reads the [purchase agreement](/orland-park/purchase-agreement-lawyer) line by line, checking contingency deadlines, earnest money terms, disclosure obligations, and any riders attached by the seller's side. If the contract contains one-sided language, unclear proration terms, or missing contingencies, your attorney can propose changes during the review period before you are locked in.

### Coordinating Title Work and Resolving Title Issues

Once under contract, a title company issues a title commitment showing the property's ownership history, liens, judgments, and easements. Your attorney reviews that commitment for problems such as unpaid property taxes, old mortgages that were never properly released, mechanic's liens, or boundary issues revealed by a survey. Clearing these problems before closing is far easier than discovering them afterward, and if serious title defects surface, our [title issues attorney](/orland-park/title-issues-attorney) work often becomes the difference between a closing that happens on schedule and one that falls apart.

### Working With Your Lender and the Closing Disclosure

Your attorney also coordinates with your mortgage lender, the seller's attorney, and the title company to make sure the numbers in your Closing Disclosure match what was negotiated, including seller credits, prorated property taxes, and any repair allowances. Small errors in these figures can cost buyers real money at the closing table, and catching them requires someone actively comparing the Closing Disclosure against the contract, not just signing where indicated.

## The Risks of Buying a Home Without a Lawyer

Some buyers, particularly in a competitive market, are tempted to skip legal representation to save a few hundred dollars or to move faster. That approach can carry real risk. Without an attorney, a buyer might miss a contingency deadline that forfeits their earnest money, agree to contract language that limits their remedies if the seller fails to disclose known defects, or close on a property with a title defect that surfaces years later when they try to sell or refinance.

Earnest money disputes are a common example. If a deal falls apart, whether the buyer gets that deposit back often depends on exactly how the contract's contingencies were worded and whether deadlines were properly documented. An attorney who was involved from the start is in a far better position to protect that money than one brought in after a dispute has already begun.

## When Hiring a Real Estate Attorney Is Especially Important

While we recommend legal representation for every purchase, certain transaction types carry higher stakes and make an attorney's involvement even more valuable.

### New Construction Purchases

Builder contracts for new construction are typically drafted entirely in the builder's favor, with limited warranties, broad rights to substitute materials, and strict deadlines for buyer objections. These agreements look nothing like the standard resale contract and deserve careful review before signing, not after.

### Short Sales and Distressed Properties

Short sales require lender approval in addition to seller agreement, and timelines can stretch for months with little communication. Our [short sale attorney](/orland-park/short-sale-attorney) team helps buyers understand realistic timing expectations and structure contracts that protect their earnest money if lender approval never comes through.

### For Sale By Owner (FSBO) Transactions

When a seller is not using a real estate agent, there is no one drafting or reviewing the contract on the seller's side either, which means important protections and disclosures can easily be left out entirely. Buyers purchasing directly from an owner benefit significantly from having their own attorney draft or heavily revise the contract from the ground up.

## Earnest Money and Contingencies

Earnest money is the deposit a buyer submits shortly after a contract is signed to demonstrate good faith, and it is usually held in escrow by the listing broker or the title company until closing. In the southern Chicago suburbs, earnest money deposits commonly range from one to a few percent of the purchase price, though the exact amount is negotiable. What matters most is not the amount itself but the contract language governing when that money is refundable.

Standard contingencies, including financing, appraisal, and inspection, each carry their own deadline and their own rules for what happens if the buyer needs to terminate. A financing contingency, for example, typically allows a buyer to cancel and recover earnest money if they are unable to secure a mortgage despite acting in good faith, while missing a deadline without formally exercising a contingency can leave that money at risk. Your attorney tracks these deadlines closely, since a single missed date can be the difference between a full refund and a forfeited deposit.

### Home Inspection Negotiations

The inspection contingency period is when most buyers get their clearest look at the physical condition of the home, and it often becomes the second major round of negotiation after the initial contract. If the inspection reveals problems, your attorney handles the formal request for repairs or credits, working within the contract's deadlines to either reach an agreement with the seller or, if necessary, exercise the right to terminate. Buyers who negotiate directly with a seller's agent, without an attorney's guidance, sometimes accept less favorable terms than the contract actually entitles them to.

## Common Mistakes Buyers Make Without Legal Representation

We regularly see a handful of avoidable mistakes from buyers who chose not to involve an attorney, or who brought one in too late to matter. These include missing the attorney review deadline entirely, agreeing to remove contingencies under pressure from a competing offer without understanding the risk, failing to confirm that a title commitment was actually reviewed before closing, and not catching a discrepancy between the negotiated repair credit and what actually appears on the Closing Disclosure. Each of these mistakes is preventable with early legal involvement, and each can be expensive or difficult to unwind once a deal has closed.

## What Happens on Closing Day

Once attorney review, inspection, financing, and title work are complete, the transaction moves to closing. Your attorney will walk you through your Closing Disclosure in advance, coordinate the final walkthrough timing, and either attend the closing with you or arrange for you to sign remotely, depending on how the transaction is structured.
