## The Typical Illinois Real Estate Closing Timeline

In most Illinois residential transactions, the closing process takes **30 to 60 days** from the date a purchase contract is signed by both parties. That said, every transaction is different. A straightforward cash purchase between motivated parties can close in two to three weeks, while a deal involving financing complications, inspection disputes, or title issues can stretch to 75 days or longer.

Understanding what happens during each phase is the best way to set realistic expectations.

### Phase 1: Contract Acceptance (Day 0)

The clock starts when the buyer and seller sign a purchase agreement. At this point, both sides have agreed on price and general terms, but several important steps still need to happen before the transaction is complete. In Illinois, this contract triggers a specific set of deadlines that are built into most standard residential agreements.

### Phase 2: Attorney Review Period (Days 1–5)

Illinois is somewhat unique because the standard residential contract includes a **five-business-day attorney review period**. During this window, both the buyer and the seller have the right to have an attorney review the contract, propose modifications, or — in some circumstances — void the agreement entirely.

This step is not just a formality. A qualified [real estate lawyer in Chicago](/chicago/real-estate-lawyer) or the surrounding suburbs can use this period to identify unfavorable terms, add protective contingencies, or negotiate changes that better reflect what the parties actually agreed to. If you are wondering whether you need professional legal help, you may want to read [Do I Need a Lawyer to Buy a House in Illinois?](/news/do-i-need-a-lawyer-to-buy-a-house-in-illinois) for more context.

Many buyers and sellers mistakenly believe the signed contract is already set in stone. The attorney review period is your opportunity to make sure the written agreement truly protects you before you are locked in.

### Phase 3: Home Inspection (Days 5–15)

After attorney review, the buyer typically has a window — often 5 to 10 days as specified in the contract — to complete a home inspection. A licensed inspector examines the property and delivers a written report identifying any material defects.

If the inspection reveals problems, the buyer and seller enter a period of negotiation. The buyer may request that the seller make repairs, provide a price reduction, or offer a credit at closing. This back-and-forth can add a few days to the timeline, but it is a necessary step to avoid inheriting unexpected problems.

### Phase 4: Mortgage Application and Underwriting (Days 1–45)

If the buyer is financing the purchase, the mortgage process often runs parallel to the inspection and attorney review phases. After signing the contract, the buyer formally submits a mortgage application (if not already done during pre-approval). The lender then:

- Orders an appraisal of the property
- Verifies the buyer's income, assets, and employment
- Reviews the title commitment
- Issues a loan commitment letter once everything is approved

**Underwriting is often the longest part of the timeline.** Lenders are processing large volumes of loans, and any document that is missing or unclear can cause the underwriter to pause and request more information. Buyers who are well-organized and respond quickly to lender requests keep this phase moving.

The type of loan also affects the timeline. Conventional loans typically move faster than FHA or VA loans, which have additional federal requirements and appraisal guidelines.

### Phase 5: Title Search and Title Commitment (Days 1–30)

While the buyer is working through financing, a title company searches the property's public records going back decades. The goal is to confirm that the seller has clear, marketable title — meaning no undisclosed liens, unpaid taxes, ownership disputes, judgments, or other encumbrances that could cloud the buyer's ownership after closing.

The title company issues a **title commitment** that outlines what it is willing to insure and lists any exceptions or requirements that must be cleared before closing. Your real estate attorney reviews this commitment to make sure there are no red flags and that all listed requirements can be satisfied in time.

Occasionally, title searches reveal issues that require additional time to resolve — for example, an old mortgage that was paid off but never properly discharged, or an easement that was never disclosed. These situations are exactly why having an experienced attorney review the title commitment matters.

### Phase 6: Final Walk-Through

Shortly before closing — typically 24 to 48 hours beforehand — the buyer conducts a final walk-through of the property. This is an opportunity to confirm that any agreed-upon repairs have been completed, that the property is in the condition expected, and that no new damage has occurred since the inspection.

If something is not right, your attorney can help you decide how to address it before you sit down at the closing table. To learn more about what happens at the actual closing appointment, read [What to Expect at a Real Estate Closing in Illinois](/news/what-to-expect-at-a-real-estate-closing-in-illinois).

### Phase 7: Closing Day

On closing day, the buyer, seller, their attorneys, and a closing agent (usually from the title company) meet — either in person or sometimes remotely — to sign a substantial package of documents and officially transfer ownership. For buyers financing the purchase, this includes the mortgage note, deed of trust, closing disclosure, and numerous lender-required forms. The seller signs the deed and various transfer documents.

Once all documents are signed, funds are disbursed, and the deed is recorded with the county recorder's office, the transaction is complete and the buyer receives the keys.

## Factors That Can Shorten or Extend the Timeline

Several variables affect how long your specific closing takes.

### Factors That Speed Things Up

- **Cash purchases:** Eliminating the mortgage process removes the single biggest source of delays.
- **Pre-approval before making an offer:** Buyers who are already pre-approved with a lender have a head start on underwriting.
- **Clean title:** Properties with straightforward ownership histories and no outstanding liens move through title review quickly.
- **Motivated, organized parties:** When both sides respond promptly to requests from attorneys, lenders, and inspectors, the timeline stays on track.
- **Experienced legal representation:** An attorney who handles Illinois real estate closings regularly knows what to watch for and can keep negotiations and paperwork moving efficiently.

### Factors That Cause Delays

- **Appraisal gaps:** If the property appraises for less than the purchase price, the buyer and seller must negotiate a resolution before the lender will move forward.
- **Underwriting conditions:** Lenders frequently issue "conditional approval," meaning they need additional documentation before finalizing the loan. Each round of conditions and responses can take several days.
- **Inspection disputes:** Extended back-and-forth over repairs or credits slows the process.
- **Title defects:** Clearing a lien, resolving a boundary dispute, or obtaining a release from a prior lender takes time.
- **Estate or probate sales:** When a seller is an estate, additional court approvals may be required.
- **Unresponsive parties:** Delays often come down to one party — a seller who is slow to provide disclosures, a buyer who cannot gather required financial documents, or a third party like a condo association that takes weeks to provide required certificates.

## What Illinois Buyers and Sellers Should Do Right Now

Knowing the timeline is only helpful if you take steps to stay ahead of each phase. Here are practical things you can do to keep your closing on track:

1. **Hire a real estate attorney before or during attorney review.** The five-business-day window goes fast. Do not wait until a problem comes up.
2. **Get mortgage pre-approval before making an offer** so your lender has a head start when the contract is signed.
3. **Schedule your home inspection immediately after attorney review ends.** Good inspectors book up quickly.
4. **Respond promptly to every request** from your lender, attorney, or title company. Delays are often caused by waiting on one missing document.
5. **Read the title commitment carefully** with your attorney so you understand what is being insured and what conditions must be met.
